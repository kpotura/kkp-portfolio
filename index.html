Leadership

Good design happens when people can do their best thinking.

01

Choose kindness

Direct feedback and high standards work better when people know they’re respected. I try to make candor useful, not theatrical.

02

Stay curious

Ask what changed. Ask what we’re assuming. Ask what the customer is doing—not just what they say they want.

03

Do good work, together

I like player-coach leadership: setting direction, clearing obstacles, mentoring designers, and staying close enough to the work to help.

04

Creativity + logic

Great product work needs imagination and structure. I use systems, research, prototypes, and measurable outcomes to turn ideas into decisions teams can act on.

“Bring structure to messy problems, make the invisible visible, and help the team move.”

I’ve led designers across enterprise products, consumer experiences, mobile, web, service design, design systems, accessibility, and AI-enabled product development.

My sweet spot is the middle of strategy and execution: translating business goals into a product direction, creating enough structure to align people, and then helping the team ship.

About Kelley

I’m a designer who became a leader without wanting to stop designing.

I’ve spent my career helping teams make complex products easier to understand and use. Along the way I’ve moved between product design, design leadership, service design, systems work, and organizational problem-solving.

I’m energized by smart, funny, generous people, ambiguous problems, and the moment a team suddenly sees a path forward. I believe the best leaders create clarity, make room for others to contribute, and keep learning themselves.

Outside work, I’m usually making something, biking somewhere, reading with the Denver UX community, or turning a half-formed idea into a project before anyone has had the chance to stop me.
